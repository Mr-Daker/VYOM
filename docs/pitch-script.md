# Judge-facing pitch script

## 20 seconds — Problem

One teacher in a multigrade classroom must teach children with different grades, learning levels, languages, and attendance histories. They have too little time to identify who needs help now, what every other group should do, and how a returning child can catch up without holding back the room.

## 20 seconds — Why now

Foundational skills build on prerequisites, so a missed step compounds quickly. Teacher attention cannot be multiplied, but current AI can adapt approved content cheaply—if deterministic rules, classroom constraints, evidence, and teacher control surround it.

## 30 seconds — Product

Saarthi-MG turns attendance and mastery into flexible need-based groups, explains which group should receive the teacher first, schedules meaningful peer and independent work for everyone else, and generates source-grounded low-resource activities. The teacher can edit, regenerate, reject, print, and approve. Exit checks then update mastery and tomorrow’s groups.

## 2 minutes — Demo

Use [demo-script.md](demo-script.md). The key story is Rajkumar: returning after missed addition practice while today’s class targets subtraction; recovery group and teacher-first support; a tiny check; transparent mastery update; automatic movement into concept launch.

## 30 seconds — Differentiation

A chatbot creates prose from a prompt. Saarthi-MG maintains learner state, traverses prerequisites, enforces group and teacher-time constraints, retrieves approved curriculum, validates materials and schema, requires teacher approval, and closes the loop with evidence. Its useful output is an executable classroom rotation—not just a lesson-plan document.

## 30 seconds — Measurable impact

On 30 fixed synthetic scenarios, all learners were assigned exactly once, every group had work in every slot, high-priority groups received teacher access, and all activities preserved competency, level, materials, and approved sources, with zero conflicts or rejected generations. These are reproducible engineering indicators. Planning-time savings and learning gains remain hypotheses for a teacher pilot.

## 20 seconds — Scalability

The MVP runs on one teacher device with printable outputs and an offline template fallback. The same modular competency graph, corpus, and policy layer can be extended grade by grade and language by language without requiring a device for every child.

## 20 seconds — Limitations and responsible AI

This is a synthetic prototype with limited curriculum and Hindi coverage, not a validated learning intervention. It stores only necessary pseudonymous learning data, makes uncertainty visible, avoids biometrics and surveillance, and leaves grouping, activities, and approval under teacher control.
